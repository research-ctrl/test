## A. Claim verification records

### NVP-01
- claim: Furuno FAR1518 is the X-band radar for the shipset. The dossier asks which IMO radar category applies.
- status: CONFIRMED (with a category condition)
- finding: Furuno describes the FAR-15x8 models (FAR-1518, FAR-1528 and their -BB black-box versions) as IMO type-approved Category 2 and Category 3 radars. The category depends on the monitor fitted. Category 2 needs an effective display diameter of at least 250 mm (Furuno lists the 19-inch MU-190 class). Category 3 needs at least 180 mm (the 15-inch MU-150HD class). A DNV certificate hosted by Furuno Norway covers FAR-1518(-BB)/FAR-1528(-BB) as Cat 2, and a separate Cat 3 certificate file and a "MED B FAR-15x8" file are hosted on the same site. Those files were seen in search results but **not opened**, so the certificate numbers and notified body are not verified. The FAR-15x3 models (FAR-1513/FAR-1523) are **non-IMO** and must not be bought for a SOLAS ship.
- regulation: IMO MSC.192(79) radar performance standards (Cat 3 < 500 GT; Cat 2 500 GT to < 10,000 GT; Cat 1 ≥ 10,000 GT; the display sizes above match the Furuno extract); IEC 62388; SOLAS V/19.2.3.2 (9 GHz radar ≥ 300 GT). The rules file is authoritative.
- sources: [Furuno FAR-15x8 product page](https://www.furuno.com/en/products/radar/far-15x8/index.html); [Furuno Norway FAR-15x8 CAT2 certificate PDF](https://furuno.no/Userfiles/Sites/files/FAR-15x8_CAT2(1).pdf); [Furuno Norway FAR-15x8 CAT3 PDF](https://furuno.no/Userfiles/Sites/files/FAR-15x8_CAT3(1).pdf); [Furuno Norway MED B FAR-15x8 PDF](https://furuno.no/Userfiles/Sites/files/MED%20B%20FAR-15x8.pdf); [Radio Holland FAR-15x8 (Cat 2 500–10,000 GT / Cat 3 < 500 GT)](https://www.radioholland.com/product/far-15x8-marine-radar/); [Furuno FAR-15x8 brochure](https://www.furuno.com/files/Brochure/310/upload/FAR-15x8_EN.pdf)
- effective: current product (the Furuno Norway shop still lists FAR-1518 24 VDC).
- applicability: Cat 3 configuration only for the 390/490 GT designs. The 770 GT design needs Cat 2, which means a 19-inch-class IMO monitor.
- implication: one radar processor serves both designs; only the monitor changes. The dossier's MU-150HD (15-inch) makes a Cat 3 set, which is **not valid for 770 GT**.
- uncertainty: MED module, notified body and certificate number not seen. Download the furuno.no MED-B PDF or check the EMSA MED database. The FAR-1418 status was not checked.

### NVP-13
- claim: "Gyro or approved non-magnetic heading … check approved equivalence" (dossier 05). The task asks whether a THD or satellite compass can replace the gyro on 770 GT.
- status: CONDITIONAL / UNVERIFIED
- finding: No IMO unified interpretation, flag circular or manufacturer approval was found that accepts a GNSS (satellite) compass as the "gyro-compass or other means" of SOLAS V/19.2.5.1. The SC-70 and SC-130 are type-approved as THD, GPS and ROTI (NVP-10), not as gyro compasses. The only equivalence evidence found concerns gyro-based arrangements replacing magnetic compasses (an Estonian flag decision, and USCG remarks on cruise-ship equivalents).
- regulation: SOLAS V/19.2.5.1, V/19.2.5.2; SOLAS I/5 (equivalents); rules file authoritative
- sources: [UK MGN 610 SOLAS V guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mgn-610-mf-amendment-1-solas-chapter-v-guidance-on-the-merchant-shipping-safety-of-navigation-regulations-2020/mgn-610-mf-amendment-1-navigation-solas-chapter-v-guidance-on-the-merchant-shipping-safety-of-navigation-regulations-2020); [USCG CSNCOE Field Notice 21-02](https://www.dco.uscg.mil/Portals/9/DCO%20Documents/5p/CSNCOE/Industry%20Notices/CSNCOE%20Field%20Notice%2021-02.pdf)
- effective: n/a
- applicability: 770 GT
- implication: **budget a gyro for 770 GT.** Without one, the saving would be about US$12–20k hardware (gyro and repeaters less SC-70). Treat that saving only as an upside if the flag accepts it in writing.
- uncertainty: Malta / BV position. Ask in writing (section E).

### NVP-30
- claim: NEW: what a sub-500 GT design can delete compared with 770 GT (task request), with A1+A2+A3 radio fixed.
- status: CONDITIONAL (depends on the rules file and flag)
- finding: Compared with 770 GT, a 390/490 GT international cargo ship (A1+A2+A3) can usually:
  - (a) **omit the gyro compass**, its repeaters and gyro distribution, using an IMO-approved THD such as the SC-70 instead (saves about US$12–20k hardware net of the SC-70, plus installation; NVP-12, NVP-13);
  - (b) fit a **Cat 3** instead of Cat 2 radar display (15-inch versus 19-inch; saves about US$1–3k);
  - (c) rely on the radar's **electronic plotting aid (EPA)** rather than an automatic tracking aid (ATA). In practice any MSC.192(79) radar already includes target tracking, so the saving is negligible;
  - (d) omit the propeller, thrust, pitch and mode indicators required by V/19.2.5.4 (saves about US$1–4k). However, a **rudder angle indicator on the bridge is still needed** if the main steering gear is power-operated (SOLAS II-1/29.13, all sizes; agent knowledge, rules file to confirm);
  - (e) carry **1 instead of 2 SARTs** and **2 instead of 3 survival-craft portable VHFs** (saves about US$1.5k).

  A sub-500 GT design does **not** avoid: radar, EPA, echo sounder, speed log, THD, AIS, LRIT, SSAS, BNWAS, daylight lamp, spare compass, or the full A1+A2+A3 GMDSS radio fit. Net hardware saving is about **US$17–27k premium**, or about US$13–24k economy (calculated from NVP-31).
- regulation: SOLAS V/19.2.2–2.5; III/6.2; IV/7–10; II-1/29.13; rules file authoritative
- sources: NVP-01, NVP-10, NVP-12, NVP-22; dossier section 05
- effective: n/a
- applicability: 390/490 vs 770 GT
- implication: equipment savings alone are modest (about 2–3 % of a small-ship build). The GT decision should rest on manning, certification and other systems, not navigation equipment.

## G. Card-ready summary (plain English, for a founder; ≤ 350 words)
- **What it is:** the bridge and radio electronics an international cargo ship of 300 GT or more must carry, plus who sells them in India and what they cost. NVP-31 gives budgets.
- **Radar category is driven by the screen.** Furuno's FAR-1518 is IMO-approved. With a 15-inch screen it is Category 3 (under 500 GT only); with a 19-inch screen it is Category 2 (needed at 770 GT). The dossier's set is the 15-inch, sub-500 GT version (NVP-01, NVP-04).
- **Old models in the dossier:** the MU-150HD monitor and SAILOR 6222 VHF are discontinued (use MU-152HD and SAILOR 7222). Furuno's new Inmarsat-C unit is FELCOM20 (NVP-04, NVP-15, NVP-19).
- **The US$54,831 Furuno figure** adds up correctly but covers navigation hardware only: no GMDSS, compasses, BNWAS, installation or gyro (NVP-11).
- **Prices:** dealer prices match most dossier figures. Several Furuno USA list prices seen were lower (radar US$10,125 against the dossier's US$12,125) (NVP-02 to NVP-10).
- **What below 500 GT saves:** no gyro (use the SC-70 satellite compass), a smaller radar screen, 1 SART instead of 2, 2 handheld VHFs instead of 3, and no propeller indicators. That is about US$17–27k in hardware (NVP-30). A rudder angle indicator is still needed.
- **What it does not save:** radar, plotting aid, echo sounder, speed log, AIS, LRIT, SSAS, BNWAS and the full A1+A2+A3 radio fit.
- **Budget estimates (agent judgement, before Indian duty and GST):**
  - 390/490 GT: about US$115–187k installed (Furuno/SAILOR) or US$79–159k (economy).
  - 770 GT: about US$135–219k (premium) or US$94–188k (economy).
  - Autopilot adds US$8–20k (NVP-31).
- **Savings to check:** LRIT through the Inmarsat-C unit (drop the separate 6130), an EM speed log instead of Doppler, and Iridium GMDSS if Malta accepts it (NVP-18, NVP-20, NVP-29).
- **Suppliers:** A.S. Moloobhoy (Furuno, self-declared exclusive). Elcome (JRC/Alphatron and SAILOR). Navcom, SJV and West Coast/Apex are unverified (NVP-25 to NVP-27).
- **Before deciding:**
  - get the written minimum equipment list from Malta and BV;
  - get MED certificates for every model;
  - get two installed quotes (Moloobhoy and Elcome) on the same specification.
