## A. Claim verification records

### BWM-01
- claim: Dossier ballast section and the task framing: the "BWM Convention 2004 (in force 8 Sep 2017)"; definition of ballast water (Article 1).
- status: CONFIRMED
- finding: The Convention was adopted in 2004 and entered into force on 8 September 2017. National implementing acts dated September 2017 corroborate the date: Norway's Regulation of 8 September 2017 No. 1368 and the Faroese/Danish executive order of 4 September 2017. Article 1.2 reads: "'Ballast Water' means water with its suspended matter taken on board a ship to control trim, list, draught, stability or stresses of the ship" (text as known to the agent; re-check against the IMO consolidated edition). The definition depends on **purpose, not source**. Sea, river, municipal or ship-generated fresh water taken on board for trim, list, draught, stability or stress is "ballast water". Solid ballast (concrete, iron) is not water, so the Convention does not cover it at all.
- regulation: BWM Convention Art. 1.2; Art. 18 (entry into force)
- sources: [Dutch Treaty Series text trb-2004-256](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/trb-2004-256.html) (search result); [Latvian official text likumi.lv](https://m.likumi.lv/doc.php?id=300134) (search result); [Univ. Oslo treaty copy](https://jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/07/7-04/imo_ballast.html) (search result); [Norway Reg. 8 Sep 2017 No. 1368](https://www.sdir.no/contentassets/b1ca5ce826af44a1b3a429dbb7b036e2/8-september-2017-no.-1368-ballast-water-management-on-ships-and-mobile-offshore-units.pdf) (search result)
- effective: 8 September 2017
- applicability: ships entitled to fly the flag of a Party, or operating under the authority of a Party (Art. 3.1). Port States apply it to ships of non-Parties with "no more favourable treatment" (Art. 3.3).
- implication: Any tank-and-pump arrangement used to trim an LCT for beaching brings the ship inside the Convention, whatever the source of the water.
- uncertainty: Article 1.2 wording was not re-opened this session.

### BWM-02
- claim: "no automatic 400 or 500 GT BWM exemption; <400 GT primarily affects certificate/survey arrangements". Dossier threshold table: "BWM discharge standard Yes* (299/390/499/770 GT); BWM certificate No* 299/390, Yes* 499/770"; "400 GT … ballast-water certificate".
- status: CONFIRMED
- finding: Article 3 sets no tonnage threshold. Regulations B-1 (BWMP), B-2 (BWRB), B-3/D-2 (discharge standard) and B-5/B-6 apply to every ship to which the Convention applies. Regulation E-1.1 limits surveys to "Ships of 400 gross tonnage and above to which this Convention applies, excluding floating platforms, FSUs and FPSOs". Regulation E-2 limits the IBWMC to ships to which E-1 applies. Regulation E-1.2: "The Administration shall establish appropriate measures for ships that are not subject to the provisions of paragraph 1 of this regulation in order to ensure that the applicable provisions of this Convention are complied with" (text as known to the agent; re-check). 400 GT is the **only** tonnage line in the BWM Convention. 500 GT has no BWM significance.
- regulation: BWM Annex regs. B-1, B-2, B-3, D-2, E-1.1, E-1.2, E-2
- sources: [UK MIN 544 (contains Convention summary)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/612888/MIN_544.pdf) (search result); [DNV BWM FAQ](https://www.dnv.com/maritime/ballast-water-management/frequently-asked-questions/) (search result; fetch blocked)
- effective: 8 September 2017
- applicability: All ships carrying ballast water, unless excluded under Art. 3.2.
- implication: **390 GT**: no IBWMC and no statutory survey cycle under the Convention, but the ship must still meet D-2 (in practice a type-approved BWMS) and carry a BWMP and BWRB. The flag will impose "appropriate measures", often BWMP approval and a national statement of compliance. **490 GT and 770 GT**: identical BWM regime (IBWMC, initial survey with commissioning test, annual/intermediate/renewal surveys). Going below 400 GT saves certificate and survey cost only, not the BWMS.
- uncertainty: Transport Malta's specific "appropriate measures" for ships under 400 GT were not verified (see BWM-21).

### BWM-03
- claim: "Article 3.2(a), (f) Not designed to carry ballast water; or permanent ballast in sealed tanks not discharged" and "Article 3.2(b)–(d) Exclusive domestic or other specified single-state/high-seas operations; relevant authorisation".
- status: CONFIRMED (wording of (a) and (f) confirmed by search snippets; (b)–(e) text as known to the agent)
- finding: Art. 3.2: "This Convention shall not apply to: (a) ships not designed or constructed to carry Ballast Water; (b) ships of a Party which only operate in waters under the jurisdiction of that Party, unless the Party determines that the discharge of Ballast Water from such ships would impair or damage their environment, human health, property or resources, or those of adjacent or other States; (c) ships of a Party which only operate in waters under the jurisdiction of another Party, subject to the authorization of the latter Party for such exclusion. One search summary placed the list under an "Article 2" heading on a Dutch page. The authentic text and the UK guidance cite **Article 3.2**.
- regulation: BWM Convention Art. 3.2(a)–(f); Art. 3.3
- sources: [wetten.overheid.nl BWBV0003219](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBV0003219) (search result); [UK MIN 544](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5f5a2dc5d3bf7f723e21c04b/MIN_544.pdf) (search result: "ships which carry permanent ballast water in sealed tanks which is not subject to discharge"); [inview.nl Article 3](https://inview.nl/openCitation/id156a343fe502bac5c7ebfd18f8a6538e/international-convention-for-the-control-and-management-of-ships-ballast-water-and-sediments-2004-artikel-3) (search result)

### BWM-04
- claim: "Regulation A-3 Emergency/accidental/same-location and other defined exceptions; not blanket exemption". Task lead: "IMO guidance on 'same location' BWM.2/Circ.52 / Rev.1 — verify".
- status: CORRECTED (the A-3 content is confirmed; the circular reference is wrong)
- finding: Regulation A-3 disapplies B-3 (and any additional measures under Art. 2.3 / Section C) for five cases: (1) uptake or discharge necessary for the safety of a ship in an emergency or saving life at sea; (2) accidental discharge or ingress resulting from damage to the ship or its equipment, if all reasonable precautions were taken and the damage was not caused wilfully or recklessly; (3) uptake and discharge used to avoid or minimise pollution incidents; (4) uptake and subsequent discharge on the high seas of the same ballast water and sediments; (5) "the discharge of Ballast Water and Sediments from a ship at the same location where the whole of that Ballast Water and those Sediments originated and provided that no mixing with unmanaged Ballast Water and Sediments from other areas has occurred". The Hong Kong Marine Department note confirms this paraphrase. **BWM.2/Circ.52 (MEPC 66, 2014) and Rev.1 are not "same location" guidance.** They are "Guidance on entry or re-entry of ships into exclusive operation within waters under the jurisdiction of a single Party", covering Art. 3.2(b)–(d), single-voyage situations such as dry-docking and mobile offshore units. Searches found **no dedicated IMO circular defining "same location"**. BWM.2/Circ.46 applies the concept to mobile offshore units' preload water. The extent of "same location" (same berth, same port, same anchorage) is a matter of national and port-State interpretation.
- regulation: BWM Annex reg. A-3.1 to A-3.5; BWM.2/Circ.52/Rev.1; BWM.2/Circ.46
- sources: [HK Marine Dept MSIN annex](https://www.mardep.gov.hk/en/msnote/pdf/msin1455anx1.pdf) (search result); [MPA Singapore PN 17-120 reproducing BWM.2/Circ.46](https://mpa.gov.sg/docs/mpalibraries/circulars-and-notices/port-marine-notices/-pn17-120) (search result); [BWM.2/Circ.52 copy (ICS Class)](https://surveyors.icsclass.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/BWM.2-Circ.52-Guidance-on-entry-or-re-entry-of-ships-into-exclusive-operation-within-waters-under-the-ju...-Secretariat.pdf) (search result); [LR Class News 10/18 on BWM.2/Circ.52/Rev.1 single-voyage](https://www.lr.org/en/knowledge/class-news/10-18/) (search result)
- effective: 8 September 2017
- applicability: All ships under the Convention. A-3.5 is an operational exception for each discharge, not a design exemption.
- implication: **A-3.5 matters for an LCT.** Ballast taken on at a beach or berth only to trim for loading or unloading, and discharged at the same place before sailing, is outside B-3 provided there has been no mixing. Ballast moved from port A to beach B is not covered. The ship still needs a BWMP and BWRB, and the flag will still expect a D-2 method for normal voyages.
- uncertainty: How Malta, India, Portugal and destination ports interpret "same location" needs written confirmation. No IMO definition was found.

### BWM-05
- claim: "Regulation A-4 Specific ports/routes; risk assessment, no unauthorised mixing, max five years with intermediate review. HELCOM/OSPAR procedure in relevant regions." Dossier asks to "verify … real exemption examples".
- status: CONFIRMED (rule); real granted examples UNVERIFIED
- finding: A-4.1 allows "a Party or Parties, in waters under their jurisdiction" to exempt ships from B-3 or C-1 only when the exemption is: (.1) for voyages between specified ports or locations, or for a ship operating exclusively between specified ports or locations; (.2) effective for no more than five years subject to intermediate review; (.3) for ships that do not mix ballast water or sediments other than between those ports or locations; (.4) based on the IMO risk-assessment guidelines (G7). A-4.2: not effective until communicated to IMO and circulated. A-4.3: must not harm adjacent States, which must be consulted. A-4.4: recorded in the BWRB. **G7**: MEPC.162(56) was superseded by **MEPC.289(71) (2017 G7)**. Its preamble records MEPC's endorsement that "the same risk area concept fits within the G7 guidelines" without further guidance, and it defines a Same Risk Area as an agreed geographical area based on a completed risk assessment. **JHP**: the HELCOM/OSPAR procedure based on G7 was amended by HELCOM HOD 59-2020 and OSPAR Agreement 2020-01 (2020 revision). Sweden applies the SRA approach alongside the JHP: ships operating solely within an agreed SRA may be exempted under A-4. An Øresund SRA (Denmark–Sweden) was designated in 2020, according to a Springer paper citing Miljøstyrelsen 2020 and a Swedish SMHI consultation dated 7 Feb 2020. The Danish Maritime Authority states that the SRA concept "developed in Denmark, has now been approved by the IMO". Any change of route or ballast-release location voids the exemption and needs a new risk assessment (Swedish Transport Agency, as cited). **Real granted exemptions:** the Helsingør–Helsingborg (4 km) route was the lobbying example (Danish Shipping, DMA, Interferry, DFDS). Scandlines commissioned a LiteHauz risk assessment for an exemption application on Gedser–Rostock. **No search result confirmed that any A-4 exemption was actually granted, or its dates.** Searches covering Spain–Morocco, Finland–Estonia, Norway and IMO GISIS notifications returned nothing.
- regulation: BWM Annex reg. A-4.1–A-4.4; resolution MEPC.289(71) (2017 G7); HELCOM/OSPAR JHP (OSPAR Agreement 2020-01; HELCOM HOD 59-2020)
- sources: [2017 G7 MEPC.289(71) (Transportstyrelsen copy)](https://www.transportstyrelsen.se/globalassets/global/sjofart/dokument/miljoskydd/barlastvatten/g7_mepc-28971.pdf) (search result); [MEPC.162(56)](https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/KnowledgeCentre/IndexofIMOResolutions/MEPCDocuments/MEPC.162(56).pdf) (search result); [Transportstyrelsen "Information on BWMC exemptions"](https://transportstyrelsen.se/globalassets/global/sjofart/dokument/miljoskydd/barlastvatten/information-on-bwmc-exemptions.pdf) (search result); [OSPAR Agreement 2020-01 (JHP)](https://www.ospar.org/documents?v=44007) (search result); [HELCOM presentation 2020 (REMPEC)](https://www.rempec.org/en/knowledge-centre/online-catalogue/3-marta-ruiz-helcom.pdf/@@download/file/3.%20Marta%20Ruiz%20(HELCOM).pdf) (search result); [SMHI Øresund SRA consultation 2020-02-07](https://www.smhi.se/om-smhi/regeringsuppdrag-och-remissvar/remissvar/remisser/2020-02-07-begaran-om-yttrande-enligt-barlastvattenforordning-sfs-201774-2-kap.-19--avseende-utpekande-av-same-risk-area-for-barlastvattenhantering-i-oresund) (search result); [Springer Biol. Invasions 2024 (Øresund SRA 2020)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10530-024-03327-0) (search result); [DMA ballast water page](https://www.dma.dk/environment-and-climate/ballast-water) (search result); [Danish EPA SRA FAQ](https://eng.mst.dk/media/xpbezoya/sra-faq-exemptions.pdf) (search result: exemption granted for five years); [Seatrade: DFDS hopeful on bilateral exemptions](https://www.seatrade-maritime.com/regulations/dfds-hopeful-on-bilateral-exemptions-from-ballast-water-convention) (search result); [Aarhus DCE: Evaluation of ballast water risk (Gedser–Rostock)](https://dce.au.dk/fileadmin/dce.au.dk/Udgivelser/Evaluation_of_ballast_water_risk.pdf) (search result)
- effective: Convention 8 Sep 2017; 2017 G7 adopted at MEPC 71 (July 2017); JHP revision 2020
- applicability: Granted by the **port/coastal State(s)** in whose waters the ship operates, **not by the flag**. For an international route both Parties must agree. Each exemption is ship- and route-specific, valid up to 5 years with intermediate review.
- implication: Realistic only for a dedicated shuttle (for example an LCT on one fixed island–mainland route) where the operator funds port biological surveys and risk assessment. Useless for a ship sold for tramp or charter trading, or for a builder's standard design. It cannot be relied on at contract stage, and a builder cannot "obtain" it for an unknown future route.
- uncertainty: No verified example of a granted exemption. Check IMO GISIS (BWM module, "Exemptions") and the Danish EPA, Transportstyrelsen and ILT (NL) decision lists. Typical lead time is 12–24 months if the JHP port-survey protocol applies (agent judgement; the JHP survey protocol was not opened).

### BWM-09
- claim: "D-1 exchange is not an unrestricted substitute."
- status: CONFIRMED
- finding: Regulation B-4 (exchange at least 200 nm from nearest land and in at least 200 m depth, or failing that at least 50 nm and 200 m depth; no deviation required; safety override) and Regulation D-1 (95% volumetric exchange, or pumping three times each tank's volume) were the interim route for existing ships. Since 8 Sep 2024 no ship may rely on D-1 alone. Exchange now survives only as a contingency measure (BWM.2/Circ.62), in combined "exchange + treatment" regimes some port States require, and in designated exchange areas (for example the Norwegian areas the NMA intends to continue). The draft revised Annex approved at MEPC 84 **phases D-1 out** and drops it from the certificate (BWM-14). For an LCT in coastal trade, B-4 distances are seldom achievable anyway.
- regulation: BWM Annex regs. B-4, D-1, B-3; BWM.2/Circ.62
- sources: [DMA guidance](https://www.dma.dk/guides/guidance-on-exchange-and-treatment-of-ballast-water) (search result); [DNV MEPC 84 summary (maritimecyprus copy)](https://maritimecyprus.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/DNV-MEPC-84-sum_c.pdf) (search result: "The D-1 standard is being phased out"); [Norway NMA ballast water page](https://sdir.no/en/shipping/vessels/equipment-and-technical-requirements/ballast-water) (search result)
- effective: D-1 no longer a standalone route since 8 Sep 2024
- applicability: All ships
- implication: Do not design on exchange. Short coastal LCT routes cannot reach 50/200 nm anyway.
- uncertainty: none for newbuilds.

### BWM-15
- claim: Route (a) "Not designed to carry ballast water … Demonstrate all loading conditions, trim, stability, propulsion immersion and beaching."
- status: CONDITIONAL
- finding: Art. 3.2(a) is an **exclusion**: if the flag accepts the ship is "not designed or constructed to carry Ballast Water", the Convention does not apply at all. No BWMS, no BWMP or BWRB, no certificate. Evidence a flag will typically want (agent judgement; no Transport Malta guidance found): a GA and tank plan with no ballast tanks, or tanks permanently assigned to other uses; no ballast pumps or sea-chest connections to such tanks; a trim and stability booklet showing **every** loading condition meets intact (and where applicable damage) stability without ballast water, including lightship and arrival with 10% consumables, cargo and no-cargo, and **beaching and ramp-down conditions**; propeller and rudder immersion, forward draught against slamming, bridge visibility and trim limits; and a statement that fresh water, fuel and cargo are not used as ballast. Ballast-free concepts are known in research and for some ferries, offshore and dry-cargo designs (for example deep-V or monomaran hulls, trim by fuel or solid ballast). No LCT-specific accepted example was found (search budget).
- regulation: BWM Convention Art. 3.2(a)
- sources: [DNV BWM FAQ](https://www.dnv.com/maritime/ballast-water-management/frequently-asked-questions/) (search result; fetch blocked); Convention text sources as BWM-03
- effective: 8 Sep 2017
- applicability: Flag Administration decision on design. It holds for any GT and any voyage.
- implication: For an **LCT** this is the hardest route. Beaching needs stern trim and later re-trim, and empty return voyages need propeller immersion. Achieving this without ballast water usually means a deeper or longer hull, more lightship (solid ballast) and less deadweight. Cost of extra steel or ballast and lost cargo against about EUR 50–80k BWMS plus lifecycle (BWT-12). It is most feasible for the **RoRo** variant with adequate light draught, or if beaching trim comes from cargo positioning. Worth a naval-architect study at 390, 490 and 770 GT because it also removes all port-State BWM exposure.
- uncertainty: Transport Malta and BV acceptance criteria. Whether using fresh-water or fuel tanks for trim would be treated as "ballast" (Art. 1.2 purpose test).

### BWT-08
- claim: "30 m³/h pump rate is not directly equivalent to oneTANK treatment capacity."
- status: CONFIRMED (engineering explanation; agent judgement)
- finding: An **in-line** BWMS (filter + UV, electrolysis) has a Treatment Rated Capacity (TRC) in m³/h that must be at least the ballast pump flow, and also has a **minimum** flow. Filter back-flushing and UVT limits can lower the effective rate, and USCG certificates sometimes set lower rated flows than IMO ones. An **in-tank** system such as oneTANK is limited by **tank volume** (maximum tank capacity in the certificate), achievable dose, **holding time** to reach the required concentration-time, and **neutraliser dosing at the deballast pump rate**. The 30 m³/h pump fixes only fill and empty time: for example 300 m³ of ballast takes about 10 h each way (illustrative; actual ballast capacity not given in the dossier). So EUR per m³/h comparisons between oneTANK and in-line systems are not like-for-like. Compare on total ballast volume, tank count, cycle time, holding time and consumables.
- regulation: BWMS Code (TRC definition); certificate conditions
- sources: dossier; agent engineering judgement
- effective: n/a
- applicability: all
- implication: Compare suppliers against the actual ballast plan: tank volumes, number of ballast and deballast cycles per voyage, minimum time between uptake and discharge on the LCT's trade.
- uncertainty: Actual ballast capacity and operating profile of BBSBD001 and the 390/490 GT variants.

- **What:** ships carrying ballast water must treat it to the "D-2" standard; new ships from delivery (BWM-01, BWM-08).
- **Size:** no 400/500 GT exemption. 390 GT avoids only certificate and surveys; 490 = 770 GT (BWM-02).
- **Real ways to avoid a system:** ballast-free or sealed/solid ballast (Art. 3.2(a)/(f)); one country's waters only (3.2(b)–(d)); port-State fixed-route exemption, ≤5 years, risk assessment (A-4). Each needs written confirmation (BWM-15 to BWM-18, BWM-05).
- **Not real routes:** potable water (US only), exchange (obsolete), emergency relief, shore reception (BWM-17, BWM-09, BWM-13, BWM-12).
- **India:** Rules of 5 Aug 2026 (G.S.R. 714(E)) start only on accession; none found (BWM-20).
- **Coming:** revised Annex due at MEPC 85 (30 Nov–3 Dec 2026), in force perhaps 2028 (BWM-14).
- **LCT issue:** in-tank chemical systems need holding time (oneTANK >24 h reported, unverified); beaching and short hops favour UV in-line systems or same-location discharge (BWT-08, BWT-09, BWM-04).
- **Cost:** historical equipment quotes EUR 46,500–76,750, plus about EUR 23,000–61,000 installation, class, commissioning, BWMP (BWT-11).

**Decision tree: do we need a BWMS?**
1. All loading and beaching conditions work without ballast water? Yes: Art. 3.2(a), no BWMS (flag letter).
2. One country's waters for life? Yes: 3.2(b)–(d) (keep retrofit space).
3. One fixed route, owner-funded risk assessment? A-4, port States decide, about 1–2 years.
4. Otherwise: BWMS with BWMS Code approval (plus USCG if US trade).
