### TON-01
- claim: "GT = (0.2 + 0.02 log₁₀V) × V ... NT concerns earning-space volume ... Statutory length is not automatically LOA." Also: Lourdes 770 GT / 231 NT.
- status: UNVERIFIED
- finding: The GT formula and the statutory-length point match the standard Tonnage Convention 1969 text as recalled, but the convention text was not opened in this session. Arithmetic check from the dossier's own numbers: 231 NT = 0.30 × 770 GT exactly, which is the regulatory floor for net tonnage (ITC 69 Reg 4: NT not less than 0.30 GT, recalled). So Lourdes' NT is at the floor, and a 490 GT design would have a floor of 147 NT and a 390 GT design 117 NT.
- regulation: ITC 69 Art. 2(8) (length), Reg 3 (GT), Reg 4 (NT) — recalled, not opened.
- sources: [IMO Tonnage Convention page](https://www.imo.org/en/about/conventions/pages/international-convention-on-tonnage-measurement-of-ships.aspx) (landing page, not opened)
- effective: ITC 69 in force 18 July 1982 for new ships (recalled).
- applicability: Ships ≥ 24 m on international voyages.
- implication: GT, not displacement, drives thresholds. Open RoRo deck spaces and excluded spaces change GT; BV or the flag measures it.
- uncertainty: Confirm measured GT and statutory L for each design with BV before freezing the general arrangement.

### NAV-01
- claim: "Spare compass, daylight signalling lamp, BNWAS — 150 GT threshold in prior matrix; verify BNWAS applicability, including new ships and flag discretion."
- status: CONFIRMED
- finding: BNWAS (bridge navigational watch alarm system) is required for cargo ships of 150 GT and upwards built on or after 1 July 2011. Existing cargo ships were phased in by size: 3,000 GT and above from 1 July 2012, 500 to under 3,000 GT from 1 July 2013, 150 to under 500 GT from 1 July 2014 (first survey after the date). Adopted by MSC.282(86); extended to pre-2002 ships by MSC.350(92). BNWAS is therefore mandatory on all three designs (390, 490, 770 GT).
- regulation: SOLAS V/19.2.2 (BNWAS); MSC.282(86); MSC.350(92); MSC.128(75) performance standard.
- sources: [US Federal Register summary of the BNWAS requirement](https://regulations.justia.com/regulations/fedreg/2013/01/14/2013-00512.html); [Standard Club regulation update](https://www.standard-club.com/fileadmin/uploads/standardclub/Documents/Import/publications/goto-handouts/2767804-loss-prevention-regulation-update-2018.pdf) (seen in search result, not opened)
- effective: 1 July 2011 for new ships.
- applicability: Cargo ships ≥ 150 GT; the flag may apply it differently to non-convention ships.
- implication: BNWAS is a fixed cost on every design; do not treat it as removable below 500 GT.
- uncertainty: Primary IMO text not opened; flag NCV Code treatment of BNWAS not confirmed.

### NAV-02

### GMD-01
- claim: Dossier §05 radio table (sea areas A1 to A4) and "India–Europe delivery is not safely assumed to be A1-only".
- status: CONFIRMED
- finding: SOLAS chapter IV was replaced by MSC.496(105), applying to new and existing ships from 1 January 2024. Sea area A3 now depends on the satellite terminal fitted (Inmarsat roughly 70°S to 70°N; for Iridium, worldwide). MF/HF is primary equipment only for A4. The alternative A3 route of VHF plus MF/HF plus a second means is no longer accepted. VHF radio and search-and-rescue locating device requirements moved from chapter III to chapter IV. The India–Europe delivery cannot rely on A1 alone.
- regulation: SOLAS IV as replaced by MSC.496(105); amendments MSC.509(105) (related instrument).
- sources: [MSC.496(105)](https://ltsa.lrv.lt/uploads/ltsa/documents/files/TJO/MSC_496(105).pdf); [Lloyd's Register class news 25-23](https://www.lr.org/en/knowledge/class-news/25-23/); [Bureau Veritas GMDSS modernisation note](https://marine-offshore.bureauveritas.com/newsroom/modernization-gmdss-entry-force-1-january-2024-amended-solas-chapter-iv)
- effective: 1 January 2024.
- applicability: Cargo ships ≥ 300 GT on international voyages (application clause recalled).
- implication: Plan A1 + A2 + A3 radio fit for the delivery voyage on all three designs (Card 08).
- uncertainty: Equipment quantities by sea area and the flag's acceptance of Iridium were not re-read.

### MED-02

- status: CONDITIONAL

### MAR-02
- claim: "Reg. 14: standard filtering equipment ≥400 GT; below 400 GT flag-approved retention or compliant discharge arrangements."
- status: CONFIRMED
- finding: MARPOL Annex I Regulation 14 frames the 15 ppm oil filtering equipment around ships of 400 GT and above. For ships below 400 GT the flag Administration must ensure they are equipped, as far as practicable, to retain oil or oily mixtures on board for discharge to reception facilities or to discharge within the regulation's conditions. In special areas a non-tanker below 400 GT may discharge only if the oil content is no more than 15 ppm. This is an alternative-compliance arrangement, not an exemption from discharge limits.
- regulation: MARPOL Annex I Reg. 14 and Reg. 15 (UK implementing text seen).
- sources: [UK Merchant Shipping (Prevention of Pollution by Oil) Regulations 2019, reg. 25](https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2019/42/regulation/25/2021-08-03/data.html); [MARPOL page](https://www.imo.org/en/about/conventions/pages/international-convention-for-the-prevention-of-pollution-from-ships-(marpol).aspx)
- effective: Existing text.
- applicability: Ships < 400 GT other than tankers < 150 GT (tankers have separate rules).
- implication: A 390 GT design avoids the oil-water separator certificate but needs retention capacity or a flag-approved arrangement.
- uncertainty: The flag's actual requirement for Malta.

### MAR-04

### ECA-01
- claim: "North-East Atlantic: MEPC.407(84), reported entry 1 Sep 2027; 0.10% sulphur from 1 Sep 2028. Tier III: contract on/after 1 Jan 2027; no-contract keel or equivalent stage on/after 1 Jul 2027; delivery on/after 1 Jan 2031."

- finding: MEPC.407(84) designating the North-East Atlantic Emission Control Area was adopted on 1 May 2026 at MEPC 84. One DNV article suggested adoption at MEPC 85, which appears to concern the ballast water amendments; the IMO documents show the ECA was adopted at MEPC 84. The exact geographic boundaries were not read.
- regulation: MARPOL Annex VI, resolution MEPC.407(84); Regulation 13 and 14 amendments.

- applicability: Ships operating in the area; Tier III by construction criteria above.

### ECA-03
- claim: "Conditional exception under Annex VI/13.5.2 for combined propulsion below 750 kW requires demonstrated design limitations and administration approval; 882 kW is not eligible on power alone."
- status: CONDITIONAL
- finding: Confirmed in substance. It is a conditional approval, not a power-based entitlement. 882 kW exceeds 750 kW, so Lourdes is outside it. Sources differ slightly in emphasis (cannot comply versus compliant engine cannot physically fit), and the current consolidated text was not opened.
- regulation: MARPOL Annex VI Reg. 13.5.2.

- applicability: Combined propulsion below 750 kW; Administration decision.
- implication: A twin 368 kW plant (736 kW) is a design change, not a loophole; it still needs flag approval and may not suit the vessel's speed and bollard-pull needs.
- uncertainty: Written flag position on what "design or construction limitations" means.

### ECA-04
- claim: "Lourdes ... may qualify under earlier construction milestones, but Baltic/North Sea trading remains an issue."
- status: CONDITIONAL
- finding: Analysis. A Tier II engine on a ship constructed on or after 1 January 2021 may not operate in the North Sea and Baltic NOx ECAs; the same applies to North America (constructed on or after 1 January 2016). - effective: See ECA-01, ECA-02.
- applicability: Ships trading in the named ECAs.
- implication: Choose the trading area before choosing the engine tier. Baltic, North Sea or North America trade needs Tier III (SCR and urea).
- uncertainty: The North Sea / Baltic and North America dates in this record come from the dossier and background knowledge, not from sources opened in this session.

### EUE-02

## G. Card-ready summary (plain English, for a founder; ≤ 350 words)
- Under 500 GT you leave most of SOLAS (certificates, ISM, ISPS) but keep radio from 300 GT and the whole of chapter V navigation, including the BNWAS bridge alarm from 150 GT.
- Under 400 GT you also avoid the MARPOL, energy-efficiency, ballast and anti-fouling certificates, but not the discharge rules.
- Emission control areas: Tier II is enough in the Mediterranean and, with a documented 2026 contract and delivery before 2031, the North-East Atlantic; the North Sea, Baltic and North America need Tier III.
- The sub-750 kW Tier III exception is a written Administration decision, not a power threshold.
- EU MRV reaches 400–5,000 GT general cargo ships from 2025; ETS inclusion is only proposed, for 2031.
- The Volvo D13 MH at 441 kW is a Rating 2 (medium duty) engine: get BV to accept it for continuous cargo service.
