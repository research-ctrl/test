### IND-01
- claim: "India: DGS; verify Merchant Shipping Act 2025 and current 2026 implementing rules, commencement and flag eligibility." (dossier §04)
- status: CONFIRMED (enactment and commencement). CONDITIONAL on one conflicting date.
- finding:
  - The Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 is Act No. 24 of 2025. It received Presidential assent on 18 August 2025 (India Code) and replaces the Merchant Shipping Act, 1958.
  - Section 1(2) lets the Central Government bring the Act into force by Gazette notification, with different dates allowed for different provisions.
  - Notification S.O. 1244(E) appointed **15 March 2026** as the date the provisions come into force. The companion S.O. 1243(E), dated 10 March 2026, brought the Coastal Shipping Act, 2025 into force on the same day.
  - India Code's legislative-history page reportedly gives "15 April 2026". That conflicts with the S.O. evidence (see section D).
  - Two independent compliance trackers and law blogs give 15 March 2026, so that is the date I trust.
- regulation: Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 (Act 24 of 2025), s.1(2). S.O. 1244(E) (MSA commencement). S.O. 1243(E) dated 10.03.2026 (Coastal Shipping Act commencement).
- sources: [India Code – MSA 2025 (search result)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/22147); [Simpliance – notification appointing 15 March 2026 as enforcement date of MSA 2025 (search result)](https://simpliance.in/India/EHS/govt_notification/Central/notification-regarding-appointing-15th-march-2026-as-the-date-of-enforcement-of-the-provisions-of-the-merchant-shipping-act-2025-8866); [TeamLease – Commencement of Coastal Shipping Act 2025 (search result)](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/53524/mopsw-commencement-of-coastal-shipping-act-2025); [Complinity legal update (search result)](https://complinity.com/legal-update/merchant-shipping-act-2025-23134/); [iPleaders – MSA 2025 (search result)](https://blog.ipleaders.in/merchant-shipping-act-2025/)
- effective: Assent 18 Aug 2025. In force 15 Mar 2026. Rules under it were notified progressively from July to November 2026 (IND-03).
- applicability: All Indian vessels, and foreign vessels in Indian jurisdiction to the extent the Act provides.
- implication:
  - Any Indian-flag option for 390, 490 or 770 GT designs must now be assessed under the 2025 Act and its 2026 Rules, not the 1958 Act and its Rules.
  - Several 1958-era rule sets are being replaced one at a time, so check the Rule in force on the contract date and on the keel-laying date.
- uncertainty:
  - I did not open the S.O. 1244(E) Gazette text.
  - The India Code "15 April 2026" entry is unexplained. It may be an India Code error, or a second commencement date for some provisions.

### IND-04
- claim: Brief: Indian ship registration eligibility (citizens, companies, LLPs, OCI, bareboat-charter-cum-demise, tonnage limits).
- status: CONFIRMED in outline (secondary sources). Details are CONDITIONAL.
- finding:
  - Ownership eligibility is in **s.15** of the MSA 2025. Commentaries say it widens eligible owners to include NRIs, OCIs, LLPs and other notified entities.
  - **s.16** allows a foreign vessel chartered on **bareboat-charter-cum-demise** (BBCD) terms by an Indian charterer to be registered as an Indian vessel for the duration of the charter.
  - A **November 2026 notification under s.15(1)** reportedly lets sea-going vessels qualify where eligible Indian owners hold a majority of the shares. A vessel is divided into 100 shares and fractional shares cannot be registered, so the majority is in practice at least 51 of 100.
  - Where an NRI or OCI holds the majority interest, an authorised representative is required.
  - A vessel wholly owned by an OCI is reportedly exempt from compulsory Indian registration, so it may register under a foreign flag.
  - The Registration of Vessels Rules 2026 (G.S.R. 756(E)) moved registration fully online.
  - I found **no minimum gross tonnage** for Indian registration of a sea-going cargo ship. Separate draft rules cover vessels below 15 tons net solely in the Indian coasting trade.
- regulation: MSA 2025 ss.15, 16 and 44. Merchant Shipping (Registration of Vessels) Rules, 2026 (G.S.R. 756(E), 21 Aug 2026). s.15(1) notification of about November 2026 (number not verified).
- sources: [Chambers – MSA 2025 Part 2 (search result)](https://chambers.com/articles/seas-of-change-understanding-the-new-merchant-shipping-act-2025-part-2); [The Week, 14 Nov 2026 – registry opened to majority-owned vessels (search result)](https://www.theweek.in/news/maritime/2026/11/14/indian-registry-for-majority-owned-vessels-merchant-shipping-act-2025.html); [Maritime Gateway – India opens registry to majority-Indian-owned vessels (search result)](https://www.maritimegateway.com/india-opens-ship-registry-to-vessels-with-majority-indian-ownership/); [Maritime Gateway – registration moves online (search result)](https://www.maritimegateway.com/india-overhauls-ship-registration-rules-moves-entirely-online-gift-city-moves-closer-to-global-ship-leasing-hub-status/); [DGMA – Registration of vessels page (search result)](https://dgma.gov.in/nautical-wing/registration-of-vessels); [India-Briefing – MSA 2025 (search result)](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/merchant-shipping-act-2025-india-business-impact-41292.html/)
- effective: Act 15 Mar 2026. Registration Rules 21 Aug 2026. Majority-ownership notification about Nov 2026.
- applicability: Sea-going vessels. BBCD registration lasts only for the charter period.
- implication:
  - An Indian owner, a 51% Indian joint venture, or a GIFT-City lessor structure can put a 390, 490 or 770 GT Bennet & Bernard (B&B) hull on the Indian flag.
  - An OCI-owned buyer can choose a foreign flag instead.
- uncertainty:
  - Exact s.15 categories and LLP and OCI conditions are not read from the statute.

### IND-06
- claim: Brief: Indian Coastal Vessel (ICV) rules and Near Coastal Voyage (NCV) categories.
- status: CONFIRMED (that the instruments exist). Scope details are UNVERIFIED.
- finding:
  - Indian coastal vessels are governed by DGS Notification 1 of 2014 / **DGS Order 01 of 2014**, "Construction, Survey, Certification and Operation of Indian Coastal Vessels" (the Coastal Vessel Rules Notification 2014).
  - **DGS Circular 54 of 2025** (safe manning) cross-refers to it and separates areas of operation into international voyages, near-coastal voyages and the Indian coast.

  - Under the new Survey, Audit and Certification Rules 2026, ICVs appear to have their own schedule (draft).
- regulation: DGS Order 01 of 2014 (Coastal Vessel Notification). DGS Circular 54 of 2025. NCV notice of 1998 (number "6 of 1998" per file name).

- effective: 2014 notification. 2025 circular.
- applicability: Indian-flag vessels on the Indian coast, plus NCV within the South Asian area.

### IND-07
- claim: Brief: IRS River-Sea Vessel (RSV) notation for coastal and sheltered waters (relevant to LCTs).
- status: CONFIRMED.
- finding:
  - The RSV regime was created by DGS Order 6 of 2010 (RSV Types 1–4; one file title refers to Types 1–5). It was superseded by **DGS Order 18 of 2013**, the RSV Notification dated 31 Jul 2013.
  - Type 1 covers ship-to-shore operation up to 12 nautical miles in fair weather only.
  - The Indian Register of Shipping (IRS) says its inland waterway vessel rules apply to RSV Types 1 and 2. Types 3 and 4 must meet the Indian flag's RSV statutory requirements.
  - IRS publishes RSV Type 1/2 and Type 3/4 reports (rev. 2).
  - DGS Order 8 of 2018 refers to inland vessels allowed in Zone 1, up to significant wave height 2 m.
  - River-sea passenger vessels fall under DGS Order 5 of 2017.
  - RSV is purely an **Indian domestic** category.
- regulation: DGS Order 18 of 2013 (RSV Notification, 31 Jul 2013). DGS Order 6 of 2010 (superseded). IRS classification notes (Aug 2013). DGS Order 8 of 2018.
- sources: [DGS Order 6 of 2010 RSV Types 1–5 (search result)](https://www.dgma.gov.in/download/1776775347_69e770b3ee62d_6-of-2010-notification-for-construction-survey-certification-and-operation-of-indian-river-sea-vessels-type-1-2-3-4-5.pdf); [Amendment noting supersession by DGS Order 18 of 2013 (search result)](https://www.dgma.gov.in/download/1776924614_69e9b7c6926fb_1-of-2013-amendment-to-dgs-order-6-of-2010-rsv-notification-superseded-by-dgs-order-18-of-2013-1.pdf); [DGS Order 18 of 2013 (search result)](https://betadgs.dgshipping.gov.in/download/1758280334_68cd3a8ef0e45_1754208470-688f18d628ee5-201311270128117344328dgsorder18-2013-b.pdf); [IRS – application of IRS rules to Indian RSV, Aug 2013 (search result)](https://irclass.org/media/3436/1-cn-application-of-irs-rules-to-indian-river-sea-vessel_aug-2013.pdf); [IRS RSV Type 1/2 report rev2 (search result)](https://www.irclass.org/media/8706/rsv-type-1-2-rpt-rev2.pdf); [IRS RSV Type 3/4 report rev2 (search result)](https://www.irclass.org/media/8385/rsv-type-3-4-rpt-rev2.pdf)

### IND-10
- claim: "India: rules reported notified 5 Aug 2026, commencement conditional on accession to BWM Convention; verify actual status." (dossier §10)
- status: CONFIRMED that the rules were notified. India's accession is UNVERIFIED (no evidence found that it has happened). The commencement wording is CONFLICTING.
- finding:

### IND-11
- claim: Dossier §07: "India / global: Generally 0.50% global sulphur cap and Tier II outside NOx ECAs; verify current local requirements."
- status: CONFIRMED (0.50% cap applied to Indian ships). CONDITIONAL (treatment of domestic coastal vessels under the 2026 Rules).
- finding:
  - DGS Engineering Circular 02 of 2019 required all vessels registered under the MSA 1958 to use fuel oil with no more than 0.50% m/m sulphur before 1 Jan 2020, unless fitted with an exhaust gas cleaning system.

  - There is no Emission Control Area (ECA) in Indian waters. That is agent knowledge, consistent with the dossier.

## G. Card-ready summary (plain English, for a founder; ≤ 350 words)
- **India's new law is live.** The Merchant Shipping Act 2025 and Coastal Shipping Act 2025 have been in force since 15 Mar 2026. DG Shipping is now the **Directorate General of Maritime Administration (DGMA)**, at dgma.gov.in. (IND-01, IND-02)
- **New Indian rules from July 2026.** The construction and fire rules (G.S.R. 590(E), 16 Jul 2026) fully cover ≥500 GT, so 770 GT gets the full regime. What applies to foreign-going 390 and 490 GT ships is still unconfirmed. Ask the DGMA. (IND-03, IND-05)
- **Domestic-only standards.** River-sea vessel (RSV) and Indian Coastal Vessel (ICV) LCTs are cheaper to build but cannot trade abroad or sail to Europe. (IND-06, IND-07)
- **Foreign-flag ships working in India need a licence.** A Malta- or Marshall-flag LCT on Indian coastal or EXIM charter needs a DGMA licence (2026 Licensing Rules). (IND-12)
- **Indian-flag, Indian-owned, Indian-built hulls win Indian tenders.** They get first right to match the lowest bid. (IND-17)
- **Ballast water rules not yet in force in India.** They were notified in Aug 2026, but India has not acceded to the BWM Convention. Export flags still require BWM compliance. (IND-10)
- **Money available:**
  - subsidy of 15% of contract value (non-specialised, ≤₹100 crore), possibly 25% if the vessel counts as "specialised" or "green" (confirm);
  - 25% yard-capex support, capped at ₹1,500 crore;
  - nil customs duty on imported ship parts to 2035.
  - Infrastructure-lending status starts at 1,500 GT, so it does not apply to any current design. (IND-13 to IND-18)
- **Non-European flags that accept 390, 490 and 770 GT:**
  - **Marshall Islands:** any tonnage in foreign trade. Under 500 GT it uses MN 2-011-8 and IACS Rec. 99. **Liberia:** possible with a tonnage waiver (below 500 NT). (FLI-01 to FLI-04, FLI-16)
  - Singapore and Bahamas reportedly need ≥1,600 GT/NT or local ownership. UAE and other Gulf flags need national owners. All unverified. (FLI-05, FLI-07, FLI-08)
- **Avoid black-list flags** (for example Togo, Comoros, Sierra Leone). Verify current MoU lists. (FLI-10, FLI-11)
