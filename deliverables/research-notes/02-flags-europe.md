### FLE-01
- claim: "Malta – Non-Convention Vessel Code Rev. 2 covers qualifying commercial cargo vessels below 500 GT …" (dossier §04); the dossier's link points to "NCV-Code-Rev-2.pdf". The task also names a "Code of Practice for the Safety of Commercial Vessels / Non-Convention Vessel Code", which suggests the two might be one document.
- status: CORRECTED
- finding: Malta has **two separate codes**.
  - (1) The **Non-Convention Vessel (NCV) Code Rev. 2** is the internationally trading code. It was issued with **Merchant Shipping Notice (MS Notice) 170**, released 14 April 2021, and has been in force since **1 September 2021**. It replaced NCV Code Rev. 1 (MS Notice 149, in force 1 January 2019). Sections 22 (passenger vessels) and 33 (survey and certification) came into force on 1 January 2023. Ships already certified had to comply by the first periodical survey on or after 1 September 2021.
  - The Malta Ship Registry's "Vessel Info" page describes Rev. 2 as the current revision. No Rev. 3, and no 2024–2026 consultation, was found.
  - (2) The **Code of Practice for the Safety of Commercial Vessels** is a different instrument. Its current edition is the **15th, dated 1 October 2025**; the 14th was October 2021. It is made under Malta's Commercial Vessels Regulations and covers commercial vessels trading **within Maltese waters**. It is not the code for an India–Europe or international trader.
  - Origin of the NCV Code: it merged the earlier Commercial Vessel Code (CVC) requirements with IACS Recommendation No. 99. Non-convention vessels under 15 m remain under the IACS Rec. 99 survey guidelines.
- regulation: Merchant Shipping Act (Cap. 234) and Transport Malta MS Notice 170 (NCV Code Rev. 2); MS Notice 149 (Rev. 1, revoked); Code of Practice for the Safety of Commercial Vessels, 15th ed. (01-10-2025).
- sources: [NCV Code Rev.2 PDF (seen in search result)](https://www.transport.gov.mt/NCV-Code-Rev-2.pdf-f6200); [MS Notice 170 page (seen in search result)](https://transport.gov.mt/maritime/merchant-shipping-notices/ms-notice-170-non-convention-vessel-ncv-code-rev-2-4651); [KPMG Malta alert 16 Apr 2021 (seen in search result)](https://home.kpmg/mt/en/home/insights/2021/04/transport-malta-revises-the-non-convention-vessel-code.html); [Malta Ship Registry – Vessel Info (seen in search result)](https://maltashipregistry.gov.mt/en-US/Technical/); [Transport Malta MS Notices list (seen in search result)](https://www.transport.gov.mt/maritime/merchant-shipping-notices-92); [Code of Practice 15th ed. ### FLE-03
- claim: NCV Code Rev. 2 "provides unrestricted navigation category". The dossier also asks: "Verify whether 'unrestricted' is subject to conditions."
- status: CONDITIONAL
- finding:
  - "Unrestricted Navigation" is defined negatively: a vessel that is **not** a "Restricted Navigation" vessel.
  - "Restricted Navigation" means the vessel may operate only in specified areas. Snippets show distance bands measured from a "safe haven", including "within 3 miles of a safe haven" and "more than 60 nautical miles from a safe haven".
  - "Safe haven" means a harbour or shelter of any kind that affords entry, subject to prudence in the prevailing weather.
  - The declared area(s) of operation, and any other conditions restricting use at sea, **must be recorded on the NCV Certificate**.
  - Vessels certified for unrestricted navigation carry **additional requirements**; snippets mention hull and side windows.
  - So "unrestricted" is not automatic. It is a certificate condition granted when the code's unrestricted-service requirements are met.
- regulation: NCV Code Rev. 2, definitions; the certification section (Section 33, from 1 Jan 2023).
- sources: [NCV Code Rev.2 (seen in search result)](https://www.transport.gov.mt/NCV-Code-Rev-2.pdf-f6200); [NCV Code Rev.1 (seen in search result)](https://www.legal500.com/developments/wp-content/uploads/sites/19/2019/05/NCV-Code-Rev-1.pdf)
- effective: 1 Sep 2021.
- applicability: All NCV-certified vessels.
- implication: for an India→Europe delivery and open-ocean trading, specify **Unrestricted Navigation** in the contract and design (LSA, radio for sea area A3, windows, stability, freeboard). Otherwise the NCV Certificate will carry an area restriction.
- uncertainty: The complete list of restricted-area bands, and the full list of additional unrestricted requirements, were not retrieved. A "30 nm" figure also appeared in one snippet. Get the code text and confirm in writing.

### FLE-04
- claim: "National equipment and length rules still apply … any length distinction around 50 m" (dossier §04 / Malta NCV detail).
- status: CONDITIONAL
- finding:
  - The NCV Code (Rev. 1 and Rev. 2) is organised in **three size bands**: **< 24 m**; **24 m to < 50 m and < 500 GT**; **≥ 50 m or ≥ 500 GT** (the last band exists for non-self-propelled and domestic vessels).
  - The ≥ 50 m / ≥ 500 GT band points to SOLAS. Example from the Rev. 1 snippet: steering gear for ≥ 500 GT must also meet SOLAS II-1 Part C.
  - Lourdes has LOA 52.68 m including the ramp and a 51 m hull, so it sits **at the 50 m boundary**. Which band applies depends on how the code measures "length" for banding: LoA, hull length, or load-line length (L).
- regulation: NCV Code Rev. 2, banded sections; SOLAS II-1 Part C cross-references for the upper band.
- sources: [NCV Code Rev.1 (seen in search result)](https://www.legal500.com/developments/wp-content/uploads/sites/19/2019/05/NCV-Code-Rev-1.pdf); [NCV Code Rev.2 (seen in search result)](https://www.transport.gov.mt/NCV-Code-Rev-2.pdf-f6200)
- effective: 1 Sep 2021.
- applicability: Maltese NCVs.
- implication:
  - A 390 or 490 GT design kept **below 50 m on the code's length definition** stays in the lighter 24–< 50 m band.
  - A ≥ 50 m design faces SOLAS-derived machinery, steering and possibly LSA/rescue-boat provisions even below 500 GT.
  - This can matter more than the 400 GT line for a 51–53 m LCT.
- uncertainty: The code's length definition used for banding, and whether a ramp counts. Written Transport Malta confirmation is essential.

### FLE-05
- claim: Malta NCV detail: "load lines, stability, subdivision".
- status: CONDITIONAL
- finding:
  - **Load line:** vessels ≥ 24 m with keel laid after 21 July 1968 (and ≥ 150 GT laid earlier) must meet the International Convention on Load Lines (LL 66). They receive a Freeboard Assignment Report and a Load Line Certificate; vessels outside those criteria receive a Freeboard Assignment Certificate.
  - For unrestricted vessels where LL 66 fully applies, **the stricter of the NCV Code and LL 66 prevails**.

  - **Stability and subdivision:** the code has stability sections, but their content was not retrieved.
  - Agent knowledge (not verified here): SOLAS II-1 Part B-1 probabilistic subdivision applies only to cargo ships ≥ 80 m (L), so a 51 m cargo ship would not face it even under SOLAS. The NCV Code and BV class may still impose damage-stability or collision-bulkhead requirements. For RoRo/LCT, BV may also apply its own damage-stability or ramp/door rules.
- regulation: LL 66 (Article 5 application, ≥ 24 m); NCV Code Rev. 2 load line and stability sections.
- sources: [NCV Code Rev.1 (seen in search result)](https://www.legal500.com/developments/wp-content/uploads/sites/19/2019/05/NCV-Code-Rev-1.pdf); [NCV Code Rev.2 (seen in search result)](https://www.transport.gov.mt/NCV-Code-Rev-2.pdf-f6200); [imorules Malta (seen in search result)](https://imorules.com/flag/malta.htm)

### FLE-08
- claim: Malta NCV detail: "Class A AIS, radar, heading/plotting, echo sounder and speed log where required. LRIT from 300 GT with applicable exception."
- status: CONDITIONAL
- finding:
  - NCV Code Rev. 2 Section 11, "Navigation Aids and Equipment", starts at about p. 112 of the PDF. Its content was not retrieved.
  - These items are in any case **convention requirements under SOLAS chapter V**, which applies to all ships on all voyages, with tonnage steps. Agent knowledge, to be cross-checked with the navigation agent (other agents hold the verified record):
    - ≥ 150 GT: spare magnetic compass, daylight signalling lamp; bridge navigational watch alarm system (BNWAS) for cargo ships constructed on or after 1 July 2011.
    - ≥ 300 GT: X-band radar, electronic plotting aid, echo sounder, speed and distance log, transmitting heading device (THD), and AIS Class A on international voyages.

- regulation: SOLAS V/19, V/19-1, V/20; NCV Code Rev. 2 Section 11.
- sources: [NCV Code Rev.2 (seen in search result)](https://www.transport.gov.mt/NCV-Code-Rev-2.pdf-f6200)
- effective: SOLAS V as amended.
- applicability:
  - 390 and 490 GT: the 150 GT and 300 GT items.
  - 770 GT: also the 500 GT items.

### FLE-09
- claim: Malta NCV detail: "Check MED and accepted alternatives". Dossier §05: "MED wheelmark applies to covered equipment on EU-flagged ships".
- status: UNVERIFIED
- finding:
  - No NCV Code text on the Marine Equipment Directive (MED), the wheel mark or type approval was retrieved.
  - Legal analysis, agent knowledge (see FLE-31): MED 2014/90/EU covers equipment on an "EU ship", meaning one flying a Member State flag *and falling within the scope of the international conventions*, where the international instruments require flag-administration approval of that equipment.
  - A Maltese 390 or 490 GT international cargo ship does fall within the scope of SOLAS chapter V, SOLAS chapter IV (≥ 300 GT), the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREG) and MARPOL. Equipment those conventions require (navigation, radio, navigation lights, and MARPOL oil-filtering equipment where required) is therefore arguably subject to MED (wheel mark).
  - Fire and LSA items carried **only** because the national code requires them are not clearly within MED. For those, the NCV Code's own wording decides: it may demand MED or SOLAS-type approval, or accept "equivalent" approval.
- regulation: Directive 2014/90/EU Art. 2 (definitions) and Art. 3 (scope); NCV Code Rev. 2 (wording unknown).
- sources: none opened (EUR-Lex blocked); searches for "NCV Code Marine Equipment Directive / wheel mark / type approved" returned no relevant text.
- effective: MED applies from 18 Sep 2016 (agent knowledge).
- applicability: Maltese-flag 390/490 GT; the 770 GT SOLAS ship falls clearly under MED for all SOLAS-required equipment.
- implication: Budget wheel-marked navigation, radio and lights for all designs. For LSA and fire equipment on 390/490 GT, get Malta's written position before buying non-MED (for example Indian Register-certified or BIS-certified) items.
- uncertainty: High. A written Transport Malta answer is required (section E, Q4).

### FLE-12
- claim: NEW: Malta registry eligibility for an Indian-owned company; age and tonnage limits.
- status: CONDITIONAL
- finding:
  - The Merchant Shipping Act (Cap. 234) qualifies Maltese citizens and bodies corporate established under Maltese law as owners. The **Ships Eligible for Registration Regulations (S.L. 234.23)** add international owners, including foreign bodies corporate.
  - A foreign body corporate must prove legal personality under its home law and appoint a **resident agent** in writing. The agent must be habitually resident in Malta. The owner may limit the agent's powers, but not those relating to registration and its maintenance.
  - So an **Indian company can register a Maltese ship directly through a resident agent**, without a Maltese company. Many owners still use a Maltese single-ship company for tonnage tax and financing reasons.
  - Age: the Transport Malta registration guide says trading ships of **20 years and over** are generally not registered, with exceptions. MS Notice 127 Rev. 3 (dated 24 April 2026) revises the seaworthiness guidelines for ships being registered. A newbuild is unaffected.
  - No minimum tonnage for registration was found.
- regulation: Cap. 234 (Merchant Shipping Act); S.L. 234.23 (Ships Eligible for Registration Regulations); MS Notice 127 Rev. 3.
- sources: [S.L. 234.23 text (seen in search result)](https://www.transport.gov.mt/S-L-234-23.pdf-f6234); [Merchant Shipping Act PDF (seen in search result)](https://www.transport.gov.mt/Merchant-Shipping-Act.pdf-f10740); [Transport Malta Guide to Ship Registration (seen in search result)](https://www.transport.gov.mt/Guide-to-Ship-Registration-Brochure.pdf-f11297); [Chambers – resident agents code (seen in search result)](https://chambers.com/articles/the-code-of-standards-for-resident-agents-strengthening-malta-s-position-as-a-leading-flag-state); [Mondaq – Malta refines seaworthiness requirements, 2026 (seen in search result)](https://www.mondaq.com/marine-shipping/1791326/malta-refines-seaworthiness-requirements-a-targeted-opportunity-for-older-tonnage); [Mondaq – New Malta flag registration requirements (title seen only)](https://www.mondaq.com/marine-shipping/1787082/industry-insight-new-malta-flag-registration-requirements)

- implication: Malta is open to Indian ownership. A foreign (Indian) body corporate is **not** a "Community shipowner" for EU cabotage (FLE-34), but it can trade internationally freely.

### FLE-24
- claim: "An international flag does not automatically authorise domestic cabotage. EU ownership and ship registration requirements under Regulation 3577/92 must be checked." (dossier §04)
- status: CONFIRMED
- finding:
  - Correct in substance, with four additions (agent knowledge, high confidence; not re-opened):
  - **(1) Who benefits.** Under Regulation (EEC) No 3577/92 Art. 1, the freedom to provide maritime cabotage applies to **"Community shipowners"** whose ships are registered in, and fly the flag of, a Member State. The ships must comply with all conditions for cabotage in that Member State.
  - **(2) Definition of Community shipowner** (Art. 2(2)):
    - (a) EU nationals established in a Member State and pursuing shipping activities;
    - (b) shipping companies established under Member State law whose **principal place of business is in, and effective control is exercised from, a Member State**;
    - (c) EU nationals or EU-controlled companies established outside the EU, if their ships fly an EU flag.
    - So an **Indian company registering a Maltese ship through a resident agent is not a Community shipowner**. Even a Maltese subsidiary qualifies only if principal place of business and effective control are in the EU (see Commission interpretative communication COM(2014) 232).
  - **(3) Manning (Art. 3).**
    - Mainland cabotage: manning is the flag state's responsibility, **except for ships smaller than 650 GT, where host-state conditions may be applied**.
    - Island cabotage: manning is the host state's responsibility.
    - Cargo vessels **over 650 GT** on island cabotage that follows or precedes an international voyage: manning is the flag state's responsibility.
  - **(4) International voyages (e.g. Malta–Italy) are not cabotage.** Intra-EU and EU–third-country carriage falls under Regulation (EEC) 4055/86 and is open.
- regulation: Regulation (EEC) 3577/92 Arts. 1, 2(1), 2(2), 3; Regulation (EEC) 4055/86; COM(2014) 232 (interpretation).
- sources: none opened (EUR-Lex blocked).
- effective: 3577/92 applicable from 1 Jan 1993 (phased derogations expired by 2004).
- applicability: EU-flag ships owned by Community shipowners.
- implication:
  - **New threshold, 650 GT.** The 390 GT and 490 GT designs (< 650 GT) can be made subject to **host-state manning rules** (crew nationality, certificates, wages) even in mainland cabotage. The 770 GT design keeps flag-state manning on mainland cabotage.
  - For international trading, cabotage rules are irrelevant.

## G. Card-ready summary (plain English, for a founder; ≤ 350 words)
- **What it is:** below 500 GT, the flag's own code replaces SOLAS construction, fire and life-saving rules. No EU law harmonises this (FLE-23).
- **Malta (best fit):** NCV Code Rev. 2, in force 1 Sep 2021. It covers international cargo ships ≥ 15 m and < 500 GT. "Unrestricted Navigation" is possible, but it is recorded on the certificate and adds requirements. An Indian company can register through a Maltese resident agent (FLE-01, 02, 03, 12).
- **770 GT Lourdes:** outside the NCV Code. Full SOLAS, ISM, ISPS and MLC certificates (FLE-11).
- **50 m trap:** the NCV Code has bands at 24 m and 50 m. A 51 m hull / 52.68 m LOA may fall in the stricter band (FLE-04).
- **No saving:** load line, SOLAS radio (≥ 300 GT), SOLAS navigation kit incl. AIS and LRIT (≥ 300 GT), MARPOL. MED wheel mark for navigation, radio and lights (FLE-05, 07, 08, 26).
- **Possible saving:** non-MED fire/LSA if the flag accepts it (unconfirmed). No ISM/ISPS/MLC certificates. No EU recycling inventory (FLE-26, 30).
- **Still applies below 500 GT:** EU port-state inspection and MLC crew standards (FLE-27, 10).
- **Cabotage:** an Indian-controlled owner cannot use EU domestic-trade freedom. Ships < 650 GT may face host-state manning (FLE-24, 25).
